This is a serious and highly impactful project. Trackers like the Council on Foreign Relations’ _Nigeria Security Tracker_ or the _Armed Conflict Location & Event Data Project (ACLED)_ have historically been vital for journalists, policymakers, and human rights workers.

To make your platform robust, credible, and genuinely useful, you will need to go beyond raw counts. Here are several features you should consider adding to elevate the website:

## 1. Deep Data Categorization

Total numbers lack context unless users can see the "who, what, and how." Breaking the data down into filterable categories will make your tracker an actual analytical tool.

|**Category**|**Data Points to Track**|
|---|---|
|**Actors Involved**|Boko Haram, ISWAP, State Security Forces, Bandits, Unknown|
|**Incident Type**|Kidnapping, Armed Clash, IED/Bombing, Raid, Arson|
|**Target Profile**|Civilians, Military/Police, Infrastructure, Schools, Religious Sites|
|**Justice Status**|Arrested, On Trial, Convicted, Repentant/Rehabilitated|

## 2. Enhanced Interactive Map Features

A map with selectable states is a great baseline, but you can push the visual storytelling further:

- **Heatmaps (Choropleth):** Instead of flat colors, states or Local Government Areas (LGAs) should dynamically shade from light to dark red based on the concentration of incidents within the filtered date range.
    
- **Cluster Mapping:** If there are 50 incidents in Borno state, a zoomed-out map should show a single bubble saying "50." As the user zooms in, that bubble breaks apart into individual pins showing exact locations.
    
- **Sub-National Granularity:** Violence in Nigeria is often highly localized. Tracking by State is good, but tracking by **Local Government Area (LGA)** is vastly better for accuracy.
    

## 3. Human & Contextual Impact

Counting deaths and injuries only tells part of the story. Terrorism disrupts entire societies.

- **IDP (Internally Displaced Persons) Counter:** Track the number of people forced to flee their homes due to specific incidents.
    
- **Infrastructure Damage Log:** Track when schools, hospitals, or critical bridges are destroyed or forced to close.
    
- **Ransom/Economic Impact:** For kidnappings (which frequently overlap with terrorism in Nigeria), tracking the number of abductees and demanded/paid ransoms paints a clearer picture of the financial engines behind the violence.
    

## 4. Transparency & Verification (Crucial for Trust)

Conflict data is highly sensitive and frequently politicized. If your site does not prove _where_ it gets its numbers, people will dismiss it as biased or fake.

- **Source Citations:** Every single logged incident must link to a verifiable source (e.g., Premium Times, Reuters, official police press releases, or NGO reports).
    
- **Confidence Scoring:** Add a tag to incidents indicating whether they are "Confirmed (Multiple Sources)," "Single Source," or "Unverified/Rumor."
    
- **Methodology Page:** Dedicate a page explaining exactly how you define "terrorism" versus "banditry" or "communal clashes," as the lines blur significantly in regions like the Middle Belt and North West.
    

## 5. Tools for Researchers and Citizens

- **Data Export (CSV/JSON):** Allow journalists, researchers, and students to download your data based on their active filters.
    
- **Automated Alerts:** Let users subscribe to specific regions. (e.g., "Send me an email alert if a new incident is logged in Kaduna State.")
    
- **Trend Graphs:** Below the map, include line charts that automatically update when the map filters change, revealing seasonal spikes or historical trends (e.g., showing if violence spikes around election seasons or dry seasons).
